## Why this matters

V314 demonstrated saturated hot-bank service in a synthetic model, but V316/V317 showed that printed SASS register tokens cannot establish physical RF traffic. A register-file mechanism must therefore survive uncertainty in RF-cache hits and physical demand before spending effort on RTL. This new executable bound rejects some proposed demand optimizations *even under their most favorable assumed demand reduction*, while explicitly refusing a positive hardware claim.

## Model and exact conditional boundary

Let s be logical source reads per instruction (an independently specified model parameter, **not** inferred from static SASS), h the physical RF-cache hit fraction, e extra physical reads per instruction, P physical RF-read capacity per cycle, W issue width and A ALU capacity. Baseline demand d = s(1-h)+e and resource-envelope rate

    lambda0 = min(W, A, P/d).

For a hypothetical candidate avoiding fraction f of the physical RF demand, with extra ALU work a, issue-control overhead c and clock-period penalty t:

    lambda1 = min(W/(1+c), A/(1+a), P/[d(1-f)]) / (1+t)
    G = lambda1 / lambda0.

The model assumes nonnegative overheads, the **same baseline RF-cache state** for candidate and comparator, no new memory/occupancy bottleneck, and that avoided reads really reduce physical port demand. It grants the candidate an optimistic RF-demand reduction without paying hardware implementation cost beyond the explicitly specified overheads.

The bound is **exact within this model** over independent input intervals: G cannot increase with h, and cannot decrease with e or f; nonnegative a, c and t cannot increase it. Thus opposite interval corners give the global extrema. The code independently enumerates all 64 vertices as a safety check.

In the zero-extra-read case, if

    h >= 1 - P / [s * min(W,A)],

the baseline is no longer RF-bound. No RF-read avoidance alone can raise the issue/compute envelope; with nonnegative overheads, G <= 1. This is a *conditional no-win theorem*, not an observed NVIDIA cache-hit threshold.

## Limitations and next gate

The capacity law is standard bottleneck reasoning, not an original GPU architecture mechanism. It ignores operand scheduling, register-cache associativity, compiler semantics, warp occupancy, wire delay, physical bank mapping, power and memory traffic. Unknown dynamic h and e make real-GPU predictions unavailable; passing the optimistic capacity gate is **necessary but not sufficient** for a physically credible improvement.

Obtain provenance-verified real compiled SASS, independently measured or bounded dynamic RF-cache behavior and actual resource utilization. Freeze real workload win/no-win/holdout predictions *before* evaluating a candidate against a modern equal-resource RF-cache-aware baseline. Promote no architecture mechanism until this is done. V287/V296 remain historical and V317 remains the sole active evidence branch and draft PR. No Kernellum branch is changed.
